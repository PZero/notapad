## Project story

notªpad is a small resin monolith, about 41 × 17.5 × 9 mm, worn around the neck. It records audio all day with two microphones, syncs with the phone over Bluetooth Low Energy and leaves transcription and note organisation to an AI chosen by the user, through an open MCP (Model Context Protocol) server.

Why we are making it: wearable AI voice recorders are a fascinating new kind of device, but almost all of them tie you to one cloud, one subscription and one AI picked by the maker. We want the opposite: open hardware, open software, and data that stays under the user's control. Transcription can even run entirely on the user's own device, with no cloud at all.

How it works: an nRF52840 module records stereo audio from two PDM microphones into a 256 MB NAND. A squeeze on both sides of the monolith (two capacitive electrodes) starts and stops recording; three RGB LEDs and a vibration motor give feedback. When you put it on its dock, four gold contacts on the back charge it and the audio is handed over to your phone, where the AI of your choice turns it into notes.

Where we are: the v0.1 board (4 layers, 1.0 mm, 16.5 × 38 mm, top-side assembly) is designed and verified, with the whole KiCad project generated by scripts. While waiting for the boards, we built the full chain on a XIAO nRF52840 Sense test bench: firmware that records only when there is sound, and an app that transcribes on the device with Whisper, separates speakers and understands voice commands. The tests are now shaping the final revision of the board.

## Who I am

We are a small team of friends who build open hardware in our spare time: electronics, firmware and software, from the KiCad project to the phone app. notªpad is a non-profit, open-source project: hardware under CERN-OHL-S-2.0, software and documentation under MIT. We enjoy designing small, well-made objects and documenting every step publicly, so that anyone can learn from it or build their own.
